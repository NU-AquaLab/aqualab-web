The Rise of Computing Colleges

Which universities have given computing its own college or school, when they did it, and how they structured it. Mostly US, with international peers included.

A university that wants to grow computing has to decide where to put it. The usual answer for fifty years was a department inside engineering or inside arts and sciences. Since 1982, the 49 institutions on this list have concluded that container is too small and have given computing its own dean, budget, and faculty lines — usually together with data science, statistics, or information science. Sixteen of them did it in 2024–2026 alone. They did not all do the same thing. Some built a standalone college; some kept computing inside engineering and gave it a school's autonomy; MIT, Berkeley, and Boston University built cross-cutting units that share faculty with existing schools rather than claiming them. This page records what each institution did, when, under what governance model, and with what money, so that the options can be compared side by side. Every entry links to its sources.
📈
The wave is 2024–2026, not 2019
Three earlier clusters — 1982–1990, 1998–2002, and 2019–2023 — each produced a handful of units. The current one has produced 16 in three years, including UT Austin, Wisconsin, NYU, Illinois, Vanderbilt, USC, and Stevens.
🏛️
Standalone is the common choice, not the only one
About two thirds of the dated entries are standalone colleges whose dean reports to the provost. The rest split between schools that stayed inside engineering — Illinois, Washington, Utah — and cross-cutting units that share faculty rather than claiming them.
🔬
The unit is rarely just computer science
The recurring merger is CS plus an iSchool plus statistics — Pittsburgh 2017, Cornell 2020, UT Austin 2026. TUM added mathematics and electrical engineering; NYU built its school around mathematics. Since 2024, AI increasingly appears in the name itself.
💰
Naming gifts range widely, and many units have none
From $10M at FIU to Schwarzman's $350M at MIT and Ann Bowers' undisclosed nine figures at Cornell. But Georgia Tech, CMU, Wisconsin, UT Austin, and Michigan Tech all built theirs without a naming donor.
