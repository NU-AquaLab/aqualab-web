## CATALOG DESCRIPTION

The Internet is a continuously evolving, loosely controlled, and highly uncooperative infrastructure of impressive scale and complexity. This seminar is intended to help you understand its evolving design, and some of the forces behind it, through active discussions and investigative projects on state-of-the-art networking research.

Throughout the quarter you will be reading and discussing recent papers in computer networks while working on two research assignments. Class meetings will be focused on a range of topics, from Internet infrastructure to censorship and privacy, with two assigned readings – a recent publication and an important, background paper in the same space. For the research part of the course, you will gain experience with the research process by reproducing some prior work.

### TOPICS

- Network design principles
- DNS and CDN
- TCP and congestion control
- Internet topology and routing
- New Internet architectures
- Software-defined networks 
- Cloud networking
- New ISPs
- Wireless and mobile networks
- Privacy and censorship

## COURSE ORGANIZATION

Most class meetings will be centered around one research topic. We will review background or motivating work during the first part of the class, and dedicate the second, and longest, part of the class to the presentation and discussion of a recent publication. You will be responsible for (one to two) conference-style papers presentations throughout the quarter.

### GRADING

I use a criterion-referenced method to assign your grade; in other words, your grade will be based on how well you do relative to predetermined performance levels, instead of in comparison with the rest of the class.

Total scores (between 0 and 100) will be determined, roughly, as follows:

 - Paper critiques 15%
 - In-class participation 15%
 - Paper presentation 20%
 - Warm-up project 15%
 - Final project 35%
