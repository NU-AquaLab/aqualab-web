Fabián gave the keynote at the **First SIGCOMM Workshop on Negative Results in
Network Measurements (NetNeg '26)** on August 17, 2026:
**"False, Unknown, and the Scientific Value of Not Knowing"**.

The talk argues that an experiment has three outcomes, not two — supported,
refuted, and undecidable on the available evidence — and that our publishing
conventions file the last two together under "didn't work." False is knowledge
about the world; Unknown is knowledge about the limits of our observation. The
damage comes from reporting Unknown as False, closing a question the evidence
leaves open.
