## Project Overview

Most of our crowdsourcing work points at the network: users' own traffic and
devices as a source of measurement about the Internet. These two apps pointed
the same idea at the city instead, and were built for the Apps 4 Metro Chicago
competitions in 2011 and 2012.

The interesting problem in both is not collection but trust. Anyone can file a
report; the question is which reports are real, which are duplicates, and which
matter enough to act on. That is a validation problem, and it looks a lot like
the one in crowdsourced network measurement, where a single client's view is
cheap and unreliable and the value comes from reconciling many of them.

---

## FixIt!

FixIt! crowdsources both the reporting and the *validation* of issues requiring
city attention — broken streetlights, potholes, graffiti. Reports from one user
can be confirmed or disputed by others nearby, so the system accumulates
evidence rather than just a queue of claims.

It took 5th place in the Apps 4 Metro Chicago Challenge, Community Round.

---

## TrailBlaze Chicago

TrailBlaze Chicago applies the same approach to cycling: planning routes and
reporting the current status of bike paths, where conditions change faster than
any official dataset can track and the people who know are the ones riding.

It came 2nd in the Apps 4 Metro Chicago Grand Challenge, and 4th in the
Transportation Round — first among the not-for-profit entries.
